A dimmer swap is one of the simplest electrical jobs there is: isolate the circuit, change the switch, put it back. It is also one of the easiest to get wrong, because the dimmer and the lamps have to be compatible.

Before anything else: in many places this is notifiable work, or must be done by a qualified electrician. Check your local rules. Nothing here is a substitute for isolating the circuit properly and testing that it is dead.

Which tier to buy

Cheapest that works

A basic trailing-edge dimmer and a standard faceplate. Fine for a single incandescent or a matched dimmable LED. Walmart →

Buy the good one

A trailing-edge dimmer rated for the total LED load on the circuit, with a proper metal faceplate. Cheap leading-edge dimmers are the ones that buzz and flicker with LED lamps. Home Depot →

Tools you probably already own

Insulated screwdriver set, Voltage tester, Wire strippers, Torch, Small container for the screws. Do not buy these new for one job.

What to skip, and why

A dimmer rated for the wrong load

Dimmers have a minimum and maximum load. Fifteen low-wattage LED lamps can sit below the minimum, so the dimmer flickers or drops out entirely. Add up the actual wattage on the circuit before choosing.

Dimming non-dimmable LED lamps

It is not a dimmer fault. Non-dimmable drivers buzz, flicker or fail early. Check the lamp packaging says dimmable before assuming the switch is broken.

Working on a circuit you have not proved dead

Switching the breaker off is not proof. Test at the terminals with a known-good tester. In a two-way circuit another conductor can still be live.

How the quantities were worked out

Add up the load before buying: eight 6 W LED lamps is 48 W total. A dimmer with a 5 W minimum and a 100 W LED maximum sits comfortably; one with a 20 W minimum would not.

Check box depth, not just the switch: a deeper dimmer body may not fit a shallow back box, which is the most common reason a swap stalls halfway.

If the wiring is aluminium, the box is full of unknown joints, or your local rules require a certificate, use a qualified electrician. This is the one page on this site where doing it yourself has a legal edge as well as a safety one.
