A heavy mirror is a static load with a lever arm. That combination is what pulls fixings out of drywall: the weight is not the problem, the weight times the distance from the wall is.

The list below assumes a mirror of roughly 10-15 kg on plasterboard, with the option of a stud if one happens to be under the hang point.

Which tier to buy

A basic toggle anchor set and a simple hook. Only if the mirror sits close to the wall and is under about 10 kg. Walmart →

Buy the good one

Toggle anchors plus a French cleat. The cleat converts two point loads into a distributed one across the back of the mirror — the single change that makes a heavy mirror safe. Home Depot →

Tools you probably already own

Drill, Spirit level, Stud finder, Screwdriver, Pencil, Small vacuum. Do not buy these new for one job.

What to skip, and why

Picture wire on a heavy mirror

Wire reduces the rated capacity of a fixing dramatically and lets the mirror shift. A cleat or direct-fixed clips are the right answer above a few kilos.

Plastic plugs in plasterboard

Same failure as with shelves: they grip the paper face, not the board. A 15 kg mirror is well outside what they hold.

Hanging on a stud only if it lands off-centre

Hitting a stud 200 mm off-centre to save an anchor is a bad trade. Use a proper anchor and hang the mirror where it belongs.

How the quantities were worked out

Two toggle anchors rated at 25 kg each give a nominal 50 kg capacity for a 15 kg mirror — a safety factor of well over three. That margin matters because drywall strength varies and the load is applied through a lever.

Use the widest horizontal spacing the mirror back allows. Doubling the spacing halves the pull-out force on each fixing.

If the wall is lath and plaster rather than modern plasterboard, treat it as a different material entirely — it crumbles around a fixing and needs a different anchor.
