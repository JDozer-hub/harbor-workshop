Painting is 70% preparation and 30% paint. If you buy only paint and a roller, you will get a finish that shows every flaw and peels at the edges within a year.

This list is sized for a typical 4 m × 4 m room with a 2.4 m ceiling — two coats, walls and ceiling, one door and one window frame.

Which tier to buy

Cheapest that works

Own-brand paint, foam rollers and a basic brush set. Honest for a rental or a spare room you are turning over. Walmart →

Buy the good one

A washable finish with a proper synthetic brush and a decent roller sleeve. Cheap sleeves shed fibres into the finish and cheap brushes leave brush marks in the cutting-in. Home Depot →

Tools you probably already own

Roller frame and tray, Synthetic brush set (including a 25 mm angled brush), Extension pole, Step ladder, Paint kettle, Rags, Gloves. Do not buy these new for one job.

What to skip, and why

Painting over a damp or stained patch without a stain block

Water stains and old smoke marks bleed through ordinary emulsion within days. A stain-blocking primer costs one extra tin and saves redoing the wall.

Caulking before filling

Caulk over a gap that should be filled shrinks as it cures and opens up again. Fill large gaps first, wait for the filler to set, then caulk the fine line.

Buying paint by number of coats on the label

Coverage depends on what is under it. Going from a dark colour to a light one, or onto bare plaster, will take more coats than the tin suggests — buy one extra litre.

How the quantities were worked out

Wall area: a 4 m × 4 m room with a 2.4 m ceiling is 16 m of wall run × 2.4 m high, about 38 m², minus roughly 4 m² for the door and window, giving about 34 m².

Two coats on 34 m² is about 68 m² of coverage. Most interior emulsions quote roughly 12 m² per litre per coat, so 68 ÷ 12 ≈ 5.7 litres of topcoat, which is why a 10 L tin leaves headroom for a third coat on the feature wall.

Ceilings: 16 m² × 2 coats = 32 m², about 2.7 litres, so a 5 L tin covers the ceiling with room for the edges.

Relative humidity and temperature change drying times significantly. Follow the tin rather than a rule of thumb.
