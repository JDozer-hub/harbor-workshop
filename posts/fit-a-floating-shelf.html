A floating shelf has no visible bracket, so all the load goes through the fixing. That is the whole job: pick the right fixing for the wall, and the rest is measuring.

The common failure is not the shelf — it is a hollow-wall fixing rated for a picture frame carrying a shelf loaded with books.

Which tier to buy

Cheapest that works

A basic anchor kit and a short level. Acceptable for a light shelf holding a plant or two. Walmart →

Buy the good one

Toggle anchors rated for a multiple of the expected load, and a long level. This is what keeps a loaded shelf off the floor. Home Depot →

Tools you probably already own

Drill with masonry and wood bits, Spirit level, Pencil, Tape measure, Screwdriver set, Small vacuum or brush. Do not buy these new for one job.

What to skip, and why

Plastic expanding plugs in hollow drywall

They grip a thin skin of paper-faced plasterboard. Under a shelf carrying real weight they pull through. Toggle or strap anchors spread the load behind the board.

A shelf longer than about 1.2 m with only two fixing points

Unscrewed length sags in the middle. Long spans need either three fixing points or a visibly thicker shelf — the bracket kit usually tells you which.

Adhesive-only mounting

Construction adhesive on paint is a wall-damage problem the day you remove it, and it is not a substitute for a mechanical fixing.

How the quantities were worked out

Find the studs first: standard stud spacing is 400 mm or 600 mm depending on the build. If two fixing points land on studs, you need no anchors at all and the shelf is far stronger.

Otherwise, allow for the worst case. A shelf holding 10 kg of books is 10 kg of pull-out force on the top fixing plus leverage from the shelf depth — so choose an anchor rated well above the load, not at it.

Load ratings on anchor packaging assume sound, correctly installed substrate. If the wall is crumbly or paper-faced, use a different fixing or a different wall.
